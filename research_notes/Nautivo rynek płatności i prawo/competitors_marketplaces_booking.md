# Competitors: boat-rental marketplaces and tour/activity booking platforms (for Nautivo)

**Verification date: 2026-11-05.** All pages were checked on this date.

**How to read the evidence labels:**
- **[DIRECT]**: the vendor's own page was fetched and read on the verification date. The fetch tool returns model-processed page text, so quotes should be spot-checked before publication.
- **[SEARCH]**: seen only in a search-result extract. The page itself could not be opened (usually HTTP 403), or was not opened.
- **[RIVAL]**: stated by a competitor or an SEO/affiliate site, so it is not neutral.
- **INFERENCE**: my reasoning, not a sourced fact.

Fees change often, and most vendor pages carry no effective date. Treat every rate as "as displayed on 2026-11-05".

## Which products exist and are active, and what do they do? (verified profiles)

### Takeaway
All the named international products exist. Neither "Tubajacht" nor "Jachtclick" could be confirmed, and "TurboTic" is not a booking product. The most direct Polish competitor found is **NaCzarter** (a Masuria marketplace with 270+ vessels), which sits alongside **Click&Boat**, **SamBoat**, **Getmyboat** and **Sailo** listings in Poland.

### Cited Findings: marketplaces

**Click&Boat** (active; global P2P and professional marketplace)
- What it does: a platform connecting owners and renters. It is "a third party to any contract entered into between an Owner and a Renter" (terms last updated 15 Oct 2025) — [DIRECT, Terms](https://www.clickandboat.com/en/terms).
- Target group: private and professional owners, and renters. Polish-language pro owner page with PLN pricing, Polish destinations and Polish operator testimonials (Sessa Charter, SieDzieje) — [DIRECT, PL pro page](https://www.clickandboat.com/pl/profesjonalni-armatorzy).
- Scale: site-wide 4.8/5 from 751,572 reviews — [DIRECT, Poland page](https://www.clickandboat.com/pl/czarter/kraj-polska). More than 55,000 boats (self-reported) — [SEARCH, Click&Boat PL](https://clickandboat.com/pl).
- Ownership: Permira-backed Boats Group completed an investment in 2021, terms undisclosed — [SEARCH, Mergerlinks](https://app.mergerlinks.com/transactions/2021-07-19-click-boat/service-providers).
- Consolidation: acquired Nautal, Scansail, Océans Evasion and Sailsharing — [SEARCH, PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/83195-92); [SEARCH, money.pl on Nautal](https://www.money.pl/gielda/platforma-czarteru-lodzi-online-click-boat-przejmuje-nautal-6537681243133569a.html).

## What do marketplaces charge owners and renters? What do booking SaaS tools charge, who pays, and how does this compare with 3% plus subscription?

### Takeaway
Marketplace owner commissions run from about **11.5% to 29%** (up to 35% implied at Boatsetter), usually with a renter fee on top. Booking SaaS charges about **1–3% plus €29–$249 per month**, or up to 6% with no subscription at FareHarbor. So **3% plus subscription is far below marketplaces but only at parity with, or above, SaaS peers** such as Rezdy and Checkfront.

### Inferences (my calculations and reasoning)
- **Nominal total take on Polish bookings at Getmyboat**, assuming non-USD: 14.5% + 16% = about 30.5 points. The two percentages may have different bases, so this is approximate. Sailo: 21–36 points.
- **Break-even against a marketplace.** Subscription S plus 3% beats commission c when monthly GMV is above S / (c − 0.03). Illustration only, with a hypothetical S of 199 zł (Nautivo's price is not given to me) against SamBoat's 20%: 199 / 0.17 ≈ **1,170 zł per month**. On that basis almost any active operator would be better off in peak months. In off-season months, a subscription may feel like a net cost.
- **Against SaaS, 3% is not a discount.** It matches Rezdy and Checkfront, and is higher than Bókun (1–1.5%), TuriTop (0.75–2%) and Getmyboat Charge on direct bookings (1.5–3%). Bookero is far cheaper for simple scheduling, but looks poorly suited to cruise and cabin logic (not tested). Nautivo needs to justify the 3% through demand, Polish payments/invoicing and sailing-specific workflows, not price alone.
- **Fee design choices that competitors have normalised:** no fee on offline bookings (Bókun, Checkfront, TuriTop, Regiondo); "pass the fee to the customer" toggles (Rezdy, Bókun, Checkfront). Operators will likely expect both.
- **Split-rate model worth considering:** Getmyboat separates marketplace-sourced from direct bookings (14.5% vs 3% international). A single 3% on both may under-earn on demand Nautivo actually generates, and may look expensive on bookings the operator brought itself.
- **Margin risk:** if 3% has to cover card/PSP fees, net revenue per booking will be thin. PSP costs are outside this note's scope; check them against the payments research.

### Gaps
- Click&Boat's actual commission: the help centre returned 403 to direct fetches, so the 26% figure should be confirmed in a browser or in an owner account.
- SamBoat 18% vs 20%: the 20% page was read directly, the 18% is from extracts. Which applies today, and whether it varies by market, is unknown.
- Getmyboat's published split on direct bookings for renters: a search extract mentioned 2.5%, but I could not trace it to a specific page, so it is not used.
- NaCzarter's commission is not public. It probably needs an owner enquiry, which is a commercial step for the user to decide on.
- FareHarbor's official terms were unreadable. Regiondo's exact per-booking fee is unpublished on the page read.

## How do they handle cold start, reviews and payments, where documented?

### Takeaway
Payments and reviews are well documented: funds are held by the platform or PSP, and payout happens around the start or end of the trip. Reviews are limited to completed rentals. Cold-start tactics are rarely documented explicitly and must mostly be inferred from acquisitions, supply aggregation and hands-on onboarding.

### Cited Findings

**Payments**
- Click&Boat: card via Stripe or Dalenys. Renter's price held in escrow. Owner paid "within 24 hours after the Rental starts", unless a complaint is filed before 2:00 PM on handover day. Deposit managed by Click&Boat or by the professional owner, as the contract specifies — [DIRECT, Terms](https://www.clickandboat.com/en/terms).
- Getmyboat: credit card only, no partial payments, full amount due on acceptance, money not transferred until trip completion. Security allowance charged only on a valid claim within 48 hours — [DIRECT](https://getmyboat.com/how-it-works/). Payouts within 48 hours after trip, to bank or PayPal — [DIRECT](https://www.getmyboat.com/for-owners/).
- Boatsetter: owners are "100% responsible for any amount disputed or charged back", with a possible $15 chargeback fee to both parties — [DIRECT](https://www.boatsetter.com/fees).
- SamBoat: deposit managed by SamBoat or the owner; optional cancellation and deposit insurance — [DIRECT](https://www.samboat.com/help/how-is-the-price-of-my-reservation-calculated-255).
- NaCzarter (Polish reference): Ideopay collects a deposit or the full price depending on owner settings, with PayU or Stripe. Any balance goes direct to the owner, and the kaucja is paid to the owner at pickup — [DIRECT](https://naczarter.pl/regulamin). BLIK, Google Pay and bank transfer are shown — [DIRECT](https://naczarter.pl/).

**Reviews**
- Click&Boat: reviews only after a completed rental or co-sailing, under a review-management policy — [DIRECT, Terms](https://www.clickandboat.com/en/terms). Two-way ratings, and owners can read a renter's sailing CV — [DIRECT, Jak to działa](https://www.clickandboat.com/pl/jak-to-dzia%C5%82a).
- NaCzarter: registered clients review charters they booked. Administrator may moderate. It also displays Google reviews and notes it does not verify whether those reviewers used its services — [DIRECT, Regulamin](https://naczarter.pl/regulamin); [DIRECT, home](https://naczarter.pl/).
- Getmyboat claims "4.9 out of 5! 500,000+ reviews" — [DIRECT](https://getmyboat.com/how-it-works/).

**Cold start (documented signals)**
- Acquisitions and cross-listing: Click&Boat's purchases of rivals — [SEARCH, PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/83195-92). Boatsetter and Getmyboat merged inventory plans — [DIRECT](https://www.boatsetter.com/boating-resources/boatsetter-and-getmyboat-announce-merger-forming-a-powerhouse-marketplace-for-boat-rentals-on-the-water-adventure).
- Supply feeds: Yachtic sources inventory through MMK and NauSYS — [DIRECT](https://yachtic.com/about).
- Hands-on onboarding: Click&Boat advisors help with listings, first bookings and fleet optimisation — [DIRECT](https://www.clickandboat.com/pl/profesjonalni-armatorzy).
- Free listing with pay-on-success, plus a managed charter option under a separate contract: NaCzarter — [DIRECT](https://naczarter.pl/dla-armatorow).
- Tools for direct bookings to hook supply: Getmyboat Charge at 1.5–3% — [DIRECT](https://www.getmyboat.com/for-owners/).
- Paid ranking via higher commission: SamBoat — [SEARCH](https://www.samboat.com/help/what-does-ad-sponsorship-mean-284?country=us).

### Inferences
- A **SaaS-first wedge** suits Nautivo: operators can adopt it for their own bookings before the marketplace has demand. Getmyboat's direct-payment tool is a partial precedent.
- A **single-niche, single-region start** has a Polish precedent: NaCzarter's Masuria-only focus reached 270+ vessels. How long this took, and at what cost, is unknown.
- Polish-specific payments (BLIK, transfers, deposit/balance split) appear to be baseline, not differentiation, since NaCzarter already offers them.
- Importing review history (Google, or operator-confirmed past customers) could soften review cold start, but needs labelling and legal checking. NaCzarter's disclaimer is a useful pattern.

### Gaps
- No platform publishes its actual cold-start playbook, or its liquidity, CAC or conversion data.
- Legal treatment of holding funds (escrow, payment-service licensing) is out of scope here; it belongs to the payments/legal research.

### Inferences
- **INFERENCE:** small organisers, clubs and skippers probably combine Facebook, forms, bank transfer and spreadsheets. The Google Forms case supports this for one student club. It does not show that commercial operators do the same. Clubs may also be unrepresentative because they run on volunteers.
- **INFERENCE:** the real competitor for many small organisers may be "free tools plus manual work" rather than any SaaS. That makes a subscription hard to sell unless it saves visible time or brings bookings. A free or low entry tier may be needed; Anolla already offers 0€ and Bókun a free plan.
- **INFERENCE:** spreadsheet use could not be sourced at all. Validate with operator interviews.

### Gaps
- No survey or market data on Polish organisers' booking tools was found. Interviews with 10–20 operators would be the cheapest way to close this.
- No source shows which Polish sailing schools take online course bookings, or which tools they use. A search for a Polish course-booking platform found none — not proof that none exists.
